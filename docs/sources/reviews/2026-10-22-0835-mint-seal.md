# Review: Mint Seal (proof of which coin contract is the official one, from minute zero)

*Project Bot review · 22 Oct 2026, 8:35am AWST (Perth time) · thesis: /workspace/idea-bot/theses/2026-10-22-0830-mint-seal.md (idea bot, 8:30am AWST, with Git bot's 8:25am addendum) · room notes: inputs-2026-10-22-0830.md*

## Verdict
- **Score: 5.5/10.** The pain is real and visible every day in our own data, but the "nobody does this" claim doesn't hold and nobody has shown they'll pay.
- **Does the gap hold up? Only partly.** The read side ("is this the CA the official X account posted?") already exists for free in LarpTrack, which records official-account CA posts every 15 minutes and grades clones. FakeWatch already sells clone-token monitoring, a public verification page and a `/verify` Telegram bot from US$39 a month. Lattice pitched almost exactly the seal flow (deployer-wallet signature or a code posted from the project's X account), and its X bot account is now suspended. Bags already ties launches to X accounts verified by sign-in. What's genuinely left is the open, portable, signed record (an attestation any wallet or bot can read), not the lookup or the alerts.
- **Biggest correction:** the thesis's own headline example is now mostly settled, and it cuts the other way. Pump.fun's public data says `CSocnz…pump` (ticker "Kimchi") was created at 7:00:15am AWST on 21 Oct, to the second the time The Defiant gives for the Kimchi mint, and lists `x.com/kimchi1x` as its X link. So our tracker very likely has Kimchi backwards (`CSocnz` is probably the real one, not a copycat). That was resolvable from public data without any seal, which weakens the "only a signature can settle it" argument. The other big risk: who pays. The closest paid product charges US$39 to US$349 a month to projects, not to bots.

## 1. Fact-check

The thesis left out that anyone, including community members, can apply for any token, and that Jupiter already warns when a ticker is verified for a different contract.
- DEX Screener Enhanced Token Info costs US$299 (shown as a sale price against US$499), one-off per token. The marketplace page itself doesn't show the price in a plain fetch; the figure comes from third-party pages checked Aug to Sep 2026 ([DXT Tools](https://dxttools.trade/dexscreener/dex-paid), [Dexsheet](https://dexsheet.com/dexscreener/)). Processing is "within just a few minutes, but please allow up to 12 hours" ([DEX Screener](https://marketplace.dexscreener.com/product/token-info)).

Git bot's rename note checks out on npm: `@solana/attestation` 2.1.0 was published 5 Oct 2026 (a placeholder 0.0.0 on 2 Oct). **Watch out:** the official docs page still tells you to install `sas-lib` (latest 1.0.10, with a 2.0.0 beta), so pin the new package and expect stale docs. - Pump.fun's "create" guide says socials are optional links and that "coins with verified X / Telegram links tend to get more attention" ([Pump.fun docs](https://pump.fun/docs/create-coin)). It doesn't explain what "verified" means there.

**Wrong, unsupported or out of date**
- **"Nobody gives a cryptographic three-way binding … in the first minutes."** Partly wrong. [Lattice's whitepaper](https://www.latticehood.app/whitepaper) describes project self-verification where "proof of control is a signed message … from the deployer or owner wallet, or a one-time code posted from the project's official account", after which `check @handle` resolves to the contract with a Verified Project badge. Its X bot (@latticehoodbot) is suspended as of this run and the site is a one-line landing page, so it looks stalled or dead. That's useful: it's the same idea, tried, with no visible traction.
- **"Risk and forensics tools … answer 'is this risky?', not 'is this the one the project launched?'"** Wrong for LarpTrack. Its methodology (draft v0.3, 17 Oct 2026) resolves the official CA from the X account in the token's own metadata, checks that account's latest CA post, grades same-ticker mints as "likely clone" (F), and runs a recorder that re-reads every official account every 15 minutes and catches deleted posts ([LarpTrack methodology](https://larptrack.xyz/methodology)). It's free, with a bot reply and a shareable card. It's small (112 followers, account created 17 Oct, has its own token).
- **Missing incumbent: FakeWatch.** Clone-token monitoring on DEXes, a public verification page, a `/verify` bot for Telegram and Discord, lookalike domains, X impersonators and takedown reports. Free plan, then US$39, US$99 and US$349 a month (the top plan is pitched at "launchpads, agencies and studios"), paid in USDT or USDC ([FakeWatch pricing](https://fakewatch.pro/pricing)). That's the thesis's "lookalike watcher" and "impersonation monitoring add-on", already priced. Its examples are mostly EVM addresses, so Solana depth is unconfirmed. Tiny X presence (6 followers).
- **Missing incumbent: Bags.** Creators verify ownership by connecting an X, TikTok or Instagram account, and the Bags API returns each mint's creator with provider and username (`/token-launch/creator/v3`) ([Bags how it works](https://bagsfmbyme.com/how-it-works), [Bags API docs](https://docs.bags.fm/how-to-guides/get-token-creators)). For Bags coins, the mint-to-X link is already proven at launch by account sign-in, which is stronger than a posted code. It's locked inside Bags (API key needed), which is exactly the "not portable" gap.
- **"Even our own coin tracker may have mislabelled it."** Understated: it very likely has. Pump.fun's public coin data for `CSocnzcRpgSCr1naKcNTieWZUxb6rABe45j844m7pump` shows ticker "Kimchi", X link `kimchi1x`, creator wallet `EFSr39…D56oF`, created 23:00:15 UTC on 20 Oct (7:00:15am AWST 21 Oct), and about US$24.7M market cap at this run. The Defiant puts the Kimchi mint at "7:00:15 pm" ET Tuesday, the same second ([The Defiant](https://thedefiant.io/news/tokens/kimchi-coin-minted-before-pump-fun-ad-fee-routing), [post](https://x.com/DefiantNews/status/2113046781458010565)). The X link is self-declared metadata, so this is strong evidence, not proof. **Action for the tracker:** flip `CSocnz` to "likely real" and re-check `9EQbGT`.
- **Kimchi's own account never posted a contract.** His last three posts (15 to 21 Oct) are a link, a vlog post and "@Pumpfun let's work" ([kimchi1x](https://x.com/kimchi1x/status/2112695417863557282)). That supports the thesis (the creator gave no official link), but note it would also defeat Mint Seal: a creator who never posts a CA won't run a seal flow either.

**The room's focus points**
- **Can a Pump.fun creator wallet sign the statement?** Yes, in practice. Pump.fun's coin data names a `creator` wallet per mint (the wallet that signed the launch transaction; The Defiant's "3-hr old wallet"), separate from the launchpad program. That wallet can sign a message, so the seal can bind to "the creator recorded by Pump.fun" rather than the mint or update authority. Two catches: the creator can be a throwaway wallet with no history (as with Kimchi), so the signature proves "same person who launched", not "the person on X"; and Pump.fun's coin API isn't a documented public API (LarpTrack notes it needs browser-like headers), so build on on-chain create transactions as the source of truth.
- **Who pays?** Still unanswered, and the evidence leans against bots paying. FakeWatch's market is projects paying monthly; Jupiter and DEX Screener charge projects one-off; LarpTrack, Larpcheck and Lattice are free (or token-funded). I found no example of a trading bot or screener paying a third party for "official CA" data. Pump.fun reportedly paying Kimchi (about US$6.5M in PUMP, unverified, X bot) shows launchpads do care which coin is "theirs", but a launchpad that already has the creator wallet and X link can badge it itself.

**Teammate signals**
- X bot: the copycat waves (Kimchi, "cybercurrency", WEFT airdrop fake) and the "only the official CA will come from this account" posts are well supported across five digests. Its point that fakes copy that line too is the best argument for a signature over a post.
- Git bot: SAS checks out (above). The "official token verification" repos are 0 to 1 stars, which shows behaviour, not demand.
- Coin tracker (Telegram): 4 of the tracker's patterns since 21 Oct are about impersonation (WEFT fake airdrop, ChatGBT and COLLECTOOOR lure pages, Kimchi copies). This is the strongest recurring gap in our data, so the problem score is high. But see the Kimchi correction above.
- Not independently checked: Proofable, the yellowbullsol and MRALEX950 posts, the "100+ coins in 20 minutes" count (X bot's own count), and the luckylegend repo.

**Does the gap hold?** Narrowly. Lookup, clone warnings and project-side monitoring all exist. The real gap is a **neutral, open, signed record** that links a mint to its creator's X account and domain, readable by any wallet or bot without an API key, and that works across launchpads (Pump.fun, Bags, Meteora). That's infrastructure, which is hard to charge for and easy for Jupiter or a launchpad to absorb.

## 4. Build plan (2 to 4 weeks)

**Re-scope first.** Don't lead with "creators seal at launch" (Kimchi shows the creators who matter most won't bother). Lead with the **read side that combines every existing proof** into one answer, and treat the seal as the upgrade path:
1. Gather every link that already exists for a mint: Pump.fun or Bags creator wallet and X link, Bags X sign-in, Jupiter verified status, official-account CA posts (like LarpTrack), a `/.well-known/token.json` file if present, and Mint Seal signatures when available.
2. Publish the combined result as an SAS attestation with a clear proof level ("X account posted this CA", "creator wallet signed", "domain confirms").

**Suggested stack:** TypeScript on Node; `@solana/attestation` (pinned) plus `@solana/kit`; Helius or Triton RPC with a webhook on Pump.fun and Bags create transactions; Postgres (Supabase is fine); Next.js lookup page on Vercel; grammY for the Telegram bot; X API basic tier only if the reply bot survives week 3 (check the cost before committing).

**Features in order**
1. Week 1: indexer for new Pump.fun and Bags launches (creator wallet, ticker, X link from metadata, timestamp) and a same-ticker clone grouper.
2. Week 1: official-CA resolver from the X account's posts (store snapshots so deletions show).
3. Week 2: lookup page and JSON API: paste a CA or ticker, get "most likely official" with the evidence and the proof level.
4. Week 2: seal flow for creators who want the top proof level (wallet signature, X code, optional domain file), written to SAS.
5. Week 3: Telegram bot (`/ca KIMCHI` and paste-a-CA checks) and alerts to subscribers when a sealed coin's ticker gets a new copy.
6. Week 4: one integration (a launchpad badge or a Telegram trading-bot warning) and a public "unsealed high-attention coins" board.

**First 10 users**
- Ethan and our own coin tracker first: run every tracked coin through it (it would have caught the Kimchi mislabel).
- Post lookups as replies on the copycat threads X bot already finds (WEFT "which contract is real?" threads, Kimchi copies). Outreach only with Ethan's approval.
- Offer free sealing to the small real projects in the tracker (WEFT is the obvious first ask, since its holders keep asking).
- Ask 3 Telegram call-channel admins to add the bot to their groups.

**The one experiment that kills or confirms it fastest (week 1, before building the seal):** take the last 20 high-attention Solana launches from the tracker and X digests and run the resolver by hand (creator wallet, metadata X link, the X account's CA posts). If the right contract is already obvious from public data in 18 or more of 20, as it turned out to be for Kimchi, the seal adds little and the product collapses into LarpTrack, so kill it or pivot to selling monitoring to projects against FakeWatch. If public data is ambiguous or wrong in 5 or more of 20, the signed seal has a real job, and the next test is whether 2 of 5 Telegram bot or launchpad teams agree to show it. (Thresholds are our own estimates, not industry figures.)
