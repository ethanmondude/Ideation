# Review: Ledgerport (self-serve developer API for desktop Sage 50 US)

*Project Bot review · 22 Oct 2026, 2:45pm AWST (Perth time) · thesis: /workspace/idea-bot/theses/2026-10-22-1430-ledgerport.md (idea bot, 2:30pm AWST) · lens: solo, bootstrapped, Ethan builds full-stack web apps · room notes: /workspace/project-bot/reviews/inputs-2026-10-22-1415.md (they covered the candidates idea bot dropped, Agent Shelf and Handoff Dial, not Ledgerport)*

## Verdict
- **Score: 6.5/10. Thesis, not lead.** This is the first idea in the series where money is clearly already moving for nearly the same product. Conductor sells the same thing for QuickBooks Desktop at $49 per company file and shows **$44,163 MRR from 291 subscriptions, verified through Stripe** ([TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/conductor)). Rutter and Apideck treat Sage 50 US as worth building, and end users already pay Commercient $299 a month for Sage 50 US sync.
- **Biggest correction:** the timing is tighter than the thesis says. Apideck **published its full Sage 50 US integration guide on 17 Oct 2026** (5 days ago) and lists the connector as "In development" ([guide](https://www.apideck.com/blog/how-to-integrate-with-the-sage-50-us-api), [status](https://www.apideck.com/accounting-software/sage-50-us)). Once it ships, Apideck's own price table falls to **$10.99 to $12.99 per connected customer at 100 to 200 customers** ([pricing](https://www.apideck.com/pricing)), so Ledgerport's $49 is only cheaper for small teams below Apideck's $599 floor. That's the wedge: small teams, not "far cheaper than Apideck".
- **Biggest money gap:** the market is small and possibly shrinking. Sage is moving customers to Sage-hosted "Sage 50 Cloud", where **third-party software can't be installed at all** (only 7 approved apps), so every hosted migration is a customer Ledgerport can't serve. A Codat survey puts Sage 50 at 2% of US small businesses against 15% for QuickBooks Desktop, so a Conductor-quality product on Sage 50 might reach roughly a seventh of Conductor's revenue (estimate). That's still about $6k MRR, which is enough for a solo founder, but $10k needs Canada or a second ledger.
- **Second gap:** the hard part isn't Ethan's stack. The connector is a 32-bit C# .NET Framework 4.8 Windows service with a signed installer. AI coding helps, but Windows reliability is where the support load comes from.

**Wrong, unsupported or missing**
- **Sage 50 Cloud is a bigger risk than the thesis says.** Sage's own knowledge base (last changed 14 Oct 2026) lists only 7 approved third-party apps for the hosted edition, and other apps can only be "considered" ([Sage KB](https://us-kb.sage.com/portal/app/portlets/results/viewsolution.jsp?solutionid=250820164946590)). Sage's Q2 2026 results call out "continued migration to the cloud" for Sage 50, plus an AWS deal to move "connected solutions like Sage 50 to a cloud-native environment" ([earnings call transcript](https://www.alphaspread.com/security/lse/sge/investor-relations/earnings-call/q2-2026)). So the Sage 50 US desktop base Ledgerport can reach is the part Sage is trying to move away. Businesses on third-party hosts (Summit Hosting and similar) can still run a connector. The thesis's mitigation ("apply for Sage 50 Cloud approval early") is unlikely to work for a one-person company.

## 2. Competitor check
The gap the thesis claims is real today, but it's a gap in **packaging and price**, not in technology.

| Option for a small SaaS team today | Sage 50 US? | Self-serve? | Price | Threat |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Conductor](https://conductor.is/) | No | Yes | $49 per file | High if it adds Sage 50; it says "starting with QuickBooks Desktop" |
| [Apideck](https://www.apideck.com/accounting-software/sage-50-us) | Coming (guide out 17 Oct) | Yes, by card, 30-day trial | $599 floor, $11 to $13 per customer at scale | **Highest.** Ships into the same search results with a unified API and MCP |
| [Rutter](https://docs.rutter.com/platforms/accounting/sage50) | Yes | No (enterprise) | Sales | Low for small teams |
| [Commercient](https://www.bigcommerce.com/apps/sage-50-us-by-commercient/), [Zynk](https://zynk.com/integrations/sage-50-us), eBridge, DBSync | Yes | Partly | $299+ per month, or quotes | Low; they sell sync to end users, not an API |
| [Daybook](https://daybookapi.com/), [Hyperext](https://mcp.hyperext.com/) | No (UK) | Daybook yes | £39 per company | Medium; could expand to the US |
| [ARIV](https://sage-api.ariv.ca/), [Conexio](https://conexio.dev/solutions/api/) | No (Canada) | Partly | Setup fees | Blocks the easy Canada expansion |

**The angle a solo founder can win:** small SaaS teams and AI-bookkeeping startups with fewer than about 25 Sage 50 US customers, for whom Apideck's $599 floor and Rutter's contract are too much. Plus "Sage 50 US MCP" as a search term nobody owns yet. Ledgerport should lead with being the specialist (deep error guides and real Sage 50 behaviour) and accept that a team with 100+ customers will compare it with Apideck on price.

## 3. Money check
- **Money already moving (strong):** Conductor's $44k MRR is Stripe-verified for the same buyer and job on a sister platform. Commercient lists Sage 50 US sync at $299 a month. Rutter and Apideck are investing in a connector. Sage 50 US customers pay $737 to $2,130 a year for the software itself. What's missing: **no proof yet that anyone pays for a Sage 50 US developer API specifically.** Daybook is pre-launch, and X has no developer posts asking for one.
- **Unit economics (estimates unless sourced):**
  - Price: $49 per active file per month (Conductor's sourced price). After founding and volume discounts, about $40 to $45.
  - Cost to serve: about $1 to $1.50 per file per month in infrastructure at 100 files (thesis estimate, about $150 a month), plus about 3% Stripe. **The real cost is support:** if each new connection costs about 30 to 60 minutes of Ethan's time (thesis estimate of 1 ticket per connection), 15 new files a month is about 8 to 15 hours.
  - Cost to win a customer: $0 in cash (SEO, AI search, direct outreach). About 2 to 4 hours of onboarding per team (thesis estimate).
  - Cash payback: immediate in cash terms, but **30 to 90 days from signup to the first paid file**, because the SaaS team must build its integration and then get a customer to install the connector (estimate; Conductor's "free until you ship to production" exists for the same reason).
  - **My suggested fix for slow revenue:** a $99 a month minimum (includes 2 files) once a team goes to production, plus the $99 pilot deposit. This brings cash forward without hurting the comparison with Apideck's $599.
- **Market size, bottom-up (estimate):** 25,000 to 54,000 US organisations on Sage 50, less those on Sage-hosted Cloud (unknown share; I'd assume 20 to 40% and rising), times 10 to 20% that use a SaaS which would sync through an API, gives about **1,500 to 8,600 reachable files**. At $45, that's about **$0.8M to $4.6M a year** in total. Cross-checking with Conductor: a seventh of $44k is about **$6k MRR** at Conductor's level of execution after several years (estimate). This is a $5k to $10k MRR business, not a $100k one. That's a fine outcome for a solo founder and a reason big players won't rush, apart from Apideck, which only has to add one connector.
- **Wedge:** AI bookkeeping and AP-agent startups that already support QuickBooks Online and Xero, have a handful of Sage 50 prospects, and can go live in days through MCP or REST. Second: vertical SaaS teams that maintain their own Sage 50 connector (ServiceBox and OrderEase advertise one).
- **Moat:** weak but real. Switching means every end user reinstalls a connector (Conductor's "virtually no churn" is the precedent). Know-how from many real offices is hard to copy quickly. No moat against Apideck on price at scale.
- **Route to the first $10k MRR without ads or sales staff (estimate):** about 205 files, or about 35 teams × 6 files. Realistically $3k to $6k MRR by month 12 and $10k only with one AP or payments customer that has 40+ Sage 50 users, or a second ledger. A good second ledger is **Sage 50 US plus QuickBooks Desktop as a cheaper Conductor alternative**, which is a bigger market but means competing with a well-loved product. I wouldn't plan on that until Sage 50 has 20 paying teams.

## 5. Build and revenue plan (solo, bootstrapped)

**Order: a 3-day spike, then the money test, then the build.** Don't write the full product until 3 teams have paid a deposit.

**Stack**
- Connector: C# .NET Framework 4.8, x86, Windows service with an outbound WebSocket to the cloud, built with AI help. Installer with WiX. Code signing: Azure Artifact Signing ($9.99 a month) is limited to US or Canadian individuals, so Ethan likely needs an Australian business with an OV certificate at about US$100 to 300 a year (thesis estimate).
- Cloud: TypeScript (Hono), Postgres on Neon, a Postgres job queue, hosted on Fly.io or Render. OpenAPI spec, SDKs generated with openapi-ts. MCP server on the same endpoints. Stripe Billing on active files.
- Test box: a Windows VM (about US$40 a month) plus a Sage 50 Pro licence ($737 a year).

**Features, in order**
1. Days 1 to 3: the connector reads customers and invoices from Sage's sample company and creates an invoice, through a local REST shim.
2. Week 2: cloud relay and queue (writes wait if the PC is off), API keys, auth-session link for the end user, and 6 resources (customers, vendors, items, sales invoices, bills, payments).
3. Week 3: MCP server (this is the fastest route to a live customer), dashboard of connections, error catalogue with help pages, TypeScript SDK.
4. Week 4: signed installer with auto-update, Stripe billing, Python SDK, polling-based webhooks.

**Monthly running costs (estimate):** about US$100 to 200 until 100 files (VM, hosting, Neon, signing, domain), plus about US$1,000 to 1,500 one-off (Sage licence, certificate, terms and DPA template). Matches the thesis.

**Where the first 10 paying users come from**
1. AI bookkeeping and AP-agent startups that support QuickBooks Online and Xero but not desktop ledgers (found through YC and Product Hunt listings and their integration pages). Pitch: Sage 50 US in your agent this week, through MCP.
2. Teams on Conductor's public logo wall who also have Sage 50 prospects.
3. Vertical SaaS teams that advertise a home-built Sage 50 integration (ServiceBox, OrderEase and those on G2's "integrates with Sage 50" lists).
4. Developers asking on Sage's SDK forum, answered with a working guide and a sandbox link.
5. Two SEO and AI-search guides in week 1: "Sage 50 US API in 2026" and "Sage 50 US MCP".
- **Any outreach, forum replies or public posts need Ethan's OK first and go out from his accounts.**

**30-day money test (thresholds are estimates)**
- Landing page with a sandbox waitlist and a **$99 pilot deposit by Stripe link**, credited to the first invoice.
- With Ethan's OK, contact 40 teams from the list above.
- Ask each one: how many Sage 50 US customers or prospects they have, whether any are on Sage-hosted Cloud, and whether they've looked at Apideck or Rutter.
- **Confirm (start the build):** 3 or more paid deposits, each naming a real Sage 50 US customer ready to install within 30 days, and the spike creates and reads back an invoice.
- **Kill:** 1 or fewer deposits after 40 contacts, **or** more than half the named customers are on Sage-hosted Cloud, **or** Apideck ships Sage 50 US before day 30 and prospects say $599 is fine for them, **or** fewer than 5 teams say Sage 50 comes up at least monthly.
- **Pivot:** if developers won't pay but bookkeepers want AI on their Sage 50 books, test a $29 per company MCP connector sold straight to Sage 50 bookkeepers and accountants (Hyperext does this for the UK). That skips the slow "developer builds first" step.
