*"Ledgerport" is a working name, and any final name must keep "Sage" out of it. This run started from a business with verified revenue (Conductor, a QuickBooks Desktop API on $44k MRR) and asked which legacy desktop ledger nobody serves the same way. ## Executive summary
- **Problem:** Sage 50 US (formerly Peachtree) is desktop accounting that US small businesses pay $737 to $2,130 a year for. It has **no web API**. The only way in is a 32-bit .NET SDK running on the Windows PC that holds the books ([Apideck guide](https://www.apideck.com/blog/how-to-integrate-with-the-sage-50-us-api)). Every SaaS company whose customers run Sage 50 (AP automation, field service, ecommerce/orders, payroll, lending, AI bookkeeping agents) either builds and maintains its own Windows connector, ships a CSV template, or says no.
- **Product:** a Conductor-style API for Sage 50 US. The SaaS company's customer installs a small signed Windows connector once. The developer gets REST plus typed TypeScript and Python SDKs, webhooks, a hosted "connect your Sage 50" onboarding flow, plain-English errors, and an MCP server so AI agents can read and write the books.
- **Who pays and how much:** SaaS developers, self-serve by card. **Free sandbox (Sage's sample company). Production $49 per connected company file per month**, with volume pricing from 50 files. That's the same price as [Conductor](https://conductor.is/) for QuickBooks Desktop, below Commercient's $299/month per-company Sage 50 US sync ([BigCommerce listing](https://www.bigcommerce.com/apps/sage-50-us-by-commercient/)), and far below Apideck's $599/month entry plan ([Apideck pricing](https://www.apideck.com/pricing)).
- **Path to $10k MRR:** about 205 connected company files at $49. At Conductor's ratio of about 6 files per paying developer account (1,700+ businesses across 291 subscriptions), that's **about 35 paying SaaS teams**. My base case is about $5.6k MRR at month 12 and $10k at about months 16 to 20. Hitting $10k by month 12 needs 2 or 3 larger AP or payments customers with 40+ Sage 50 users each (estimates; table below).
- **Why now:** (1) Proof that this model works: Conductor makes **$44,163 MRR from 291 subscriptions, verified on Stripe, and its founder says it "completely runs itself"** ([TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/conductor)). (2) The same play launched for UK Sage 50 this month at £39 per company ([Daybook](https://daybookapi.com/)), which says US/Canada "isn't supported". (3) Rutter limits Sage 50 to enterprise contracts ([Rutter docs](https://docs.rutter.com/platforms/accounting/sage50)), Codat covers only the UK edition ([Codat docs](https://docs.codat.io/integrations/accounting/sage50/accounting-sage50)), and Apideck's US connector is "in development" ([Apideck](https://www.apideck.com/accounting-software/sage-50-us)). (4) AI agents need programmatic access to SMB books. Conductor and Hyperext (UK) have both shipped MCP servers, and nobody has one for Sage 50 US.
- **Edge (narrow; be honest):** I found at least 9 ways to connect to Sage 50 US (table below). **None is self-serve, priced per company, and aimed at developers with typed SDKs and MCP.** The others are enterprise-only (Rutter), not live yet and $599/month and up (Apideck), end-user sync products priced by quote or at $99 to $399 per company (Commercient, Zynk, eBridge, DBSync), other countries' editions (Daybook, Hyperext, Codat for the UK; ARIV for Canada), or unmaintained open-source wrappers (Masleap Sage50API).

Behind that: Sage 50 US is a smaller and possibly shrinking base (Sage is pushing a Sage-hosted "Sage 50 Cloud" that lists only seven approved third-party apps), Windows connector support load, and slow time to revenue, because buyers must build their integration before their first customer connects.

## Problem and buyer
**Buyer persona (who pays).** A founder, CTO or integrations engineer at a 3 to 50-person vertical or fintech SaaS company that sells to US small and mid-sized businesses. Examples are AP automation, expense and spend tools, field-service and construction software, B2B order management, payroll and time tracking, lending and underwriting, and AI bookkeeping agents. Their customers' books sit in desktop Sage 50 US. They pay by card for developer infrastructure (Stripe, Vercel, Conductor) and buy without procurement up to a few hundred dollars a month.

**End user (who installs the connector).** The bookkeeper or office manager at the small business who runs Sage 50 Pro, Premium or Quantum on a Windows PC or office server.

**What developers do today (sourced):**
- **Build a Windows connector themselves.** Sage 50 US offers only "a 32-bit .NET SDK that ships inside the product… Your application has to target .NET Framework 4.8 and compile as 32-bit". A cloud product "needs… a Windows service on the machine or server that holds the Sage 50 data" and must handle `CompanyLocked`, per-company approval prompts, yearly releases and data conversions ([Apideck](https://www.apideck.com/blog/how-to-integrate-with-the-sage-50-us-api)). Recompiling the app "requires the customer's admin to authorize it again" (same source). Vertical SaaS companies such as [ServiceBox](https://x.com/getservicebox/status/2064709384450568471) and [OrderEase](https://www.orderease.com/integrations/sage-50) advertise Sage 50 integrations they had to build themselves.
- **Refer the customer to an integration firm** that charges the end user: Commercient SYNC for Sage 50 US is listed at $299/month on BigCommerce ([listing](https://www.bigcommerce.com/apps/sage-50-us-by-commercient/)), and search snippets show $399 per company per month on Salesforce AppExchange and $99 on Adobe Commerce (snippets, not opened). Zynk and eBridge quote per project ([Zynk](https://zynk.com/integrations/sage-50-us), [eBridge](https://us-marketplace.sage.com/en-us/apps/53126/ebridge-connections-for-sage)).
- **Use read-only ODBC or CSV.** ODBC is read-only and doesn't work with 64-bit programs. CSV import "fails if the file's fields don't exactly match" (Apideck).
- **Say no.** Codat's US list has no Sage 50 ([Codat](https://docs.codat.io/integrations/accounting/overview)). Merge and Unified.to don't list it (Unified.to covers QuickBooks Desktop and Sage 100) ([Unified.to](https://unified.to/blog/chift_vs_unified_which_unified_api_should_your_product_use_in_2026), [Merge](https://www.merge.dev/categories/accounting-api)).

**What it costs them (estimates, labelled):** a first Windows connector is about 4 to 8 engineer-weeks, plus about 1 to 3 days per Sage annual release, plus support tickets for locks, permissions and approvals (estimate, based on the integration steps Apideck lists). At a US$150k loaded engineer cost, the first build is about US$12k to 23k (estimate). Ledgerport at $49 per company only costs more than that once a SaaS has about 20+ Sage 50 customers for 2 years (arithmetic: 20 × $49 × 24 = $23.5k). Even then, they're buying it to avoid the maintenance and support work, not the first build.

## Product
**MVP (weeks 1 to 4), web-first, with one Windows component:**
- **Windows connector** (C#, .NET Framework 4.8, x86, installed as a Windows service): `PeachtreeSession.Begin(appId)` → company list → `RequestAccess`. It handles the approval prompt with an on-screen guide, polls an outbound HTTPS/WebSocket queue (no inbound ports), executes jobs, returns Sage's IDs, retries on `CompanyLocked`, and keeps no accounting data on the server beyond a request log.
- **Cloud API** (Ethan's stack): TypeScript (Hono or Next.js API routes), Postgres (Neon), a job queue (Redis or Postgres), REST endpoints for customers, vendors, items, sales invoices, purchase invoices/bills, receipts/payments, chart of accounts, jobs, and read-only GL transactions. Webhooks come from polling diffs.
- **Developer surface:** API keys, a dashboard of end-user connections with live status, an "auth session" URL that the SaaS sends its customer (download → install → approve → connected), typed SDKs generated from OpenAPI (Stainless or openapi-ts), and an MCP server built on the same endpoints.
- **Error catalogue:** every Sage error mapped to a user-facing message and help article (Conductor's strongest feature, per its testimonials).

**Week by week:**
1. Week 1: Windows VM, Sage 50 sample company, connector proof (read customers and invoices; create an invoice) through a local REST shim. Request a free application ID from sdk.50us@sage.com ([Sage staff: no cost](https://communityhub.sage.com/us/sage50_us/f/software-development-kit-sdk/271035/sage-50-sdk-application-id)).
2. Week 2: cloud relay plus queue, auth sessions, API keys, the first 6 resources read and write, and a signed installer (MSI).
3. Week 3: dashboard, webhooks (polling diffs), TypeScript and Python SDKs, docs site, error catalogue v1, MCP server.
4. Week 4: Stripe billing per active company file, free sandbox, help guides (approval prompt, security level, SDK Data Access, locks), and 2 design partners live on the sandbox.

**What's hard:** (a) Sage 50 quirks: cash-basis companies can't create invoices through the SDK, units of measure and serialized inventory are limited, SDK-created sales invoices make no audit-trail entries, and only one company can be open at a time (all from the Apideck guide). (b) Windows reliability: PCs that sleep, locked files during backups, AV software flagging the installer. (c) Yearly Sage releases need a test pass before customers upgrade. (d) Sage 50 Cloud (Sage-hosted): third-party apps need Sage's approval, so at launch it isn't supported.

**What a copycat clones in a weekend:** the REST wrapper (open-source ones exist). **What it can't clone quickly:** a signed, self-updating connector that has survived a few hundred real offices, the error catalogue and help guides, release testing, and SaaS teams that have already wired the SDK into their product (switching means re-integrating).

## Defensibility
- **Integration lock-in:** once a SaaS has wired the SDK and its customers have installed the connector, switching means re-onboarding every end user. Conductor's "virtually no churn" is the precedent.
- **Operational know-how:** the error catalogue, release testing and help guides build up with every real office. That's slow for a newcomer to copy, though not a moat against a funded unified API.
- **Distribution in search:** owning "Sage 50 API" and "Sage 50 MCP" in Google and AI answers. That's cheap to build now, while few people write for this.
- **Weak spots:** no network effects. Apideck, Rutter or a buyer of Conductor could match the features. Defensibility is mostly being first, cheap and well run in a niche too small for funded players to prioritise.

## Kill or confirm: 30-day paid validation
**Plan:** spike the connector (days 1 to 3), put up the landing page and sandbox waitlist, and contact 40 SaaS teams (Conductor customers, vertical SaaS with Sage 50 integrations, AI bookkeeping startups). **Outreach needs Ethan's OK and goes from his accounts.**
- **Confirm (build weeks 2 to 4):** at least **10 discovery calls**, at least **3 teams pay a $99 pilot deposit by card** (credited to the first invoice), and each names at least 1 real Sage 50 US customer ready to install within 30 days. The technical spike also has to create and read back an invoice in the sample company.
- **Kill:** at most 1 deposit after 40 contacts, **or** fewer than 5 teams say Sage 50 US comes up in their sales or support at least monthly, **or** Apideck ships a Sage 50 US connector at under $150 a month, **or** Sage refuses an application ID.
- **Pivot signals:** if teams want it but only for Canada or the UK, re-check ARIV and Daybook first. If the demand is for an AI assistant on Sage 50 data rather than an API, test a $29 per company MCP-only product sold to bookkeepers and accountants (Hyperext has the UK version).

- **Daybook** (Sage 50 UK API): £39 per company, early access, one-person firm, Oct 2026 ([daybookapi.com](https://daybookapi.com/)). Pre-revenue as far as is public.
- **Hyperext** (Sage 50 UK API and MCP): "15+ years of Sage 50 integration, 2,000+ companies connected"; £900 setup plus £600 a year ([Hyperext](https://mcp.hyperext.com/)).
- **Coolify** (different market, same solo-founder pattern of charging for convenience on top of something free): about $23k hosted MRR and 3,000+ cloud users, per coolLabs' own figures as reported on 12 Oct 2026 ([tsukureruai](https://tsukureruai.net/article/coolify-exact-env-compatibility/), [Open Collective](https://opencollective.com/coollabsio)).
- **Sale multiples:** recent TrustMRR listings ask 3.9× to 5.2× for small apps ([trustmrr.com](https://trustmrr.com/)). A $10k MRR API with low churn would plausibly ask $400k to $600k (estimate, not a valuation).

"Get onto the pages ChatGPT cites" outreach is also taken (AutoBacklinks, Targetlytics, Omnia, ListBrew, Agentik).
- **Railway exit / "Railway UX on cheap servers":** crowded. Render pays up to $10,000 in migration credits and has a Railway guide ([Render](https://render.com/migration-credits)). Railway importers already exist (Skiffly, Forge, Cloady), plus Coolify Cloud $5, Dokploy Cloud $4.50/server, Sliplane (about 1,100 paying customers, bootstrapped), Temps, and migration services (wz-it). Money moves to hosts, not to a migration layer, and migration is one-off.
- **Python stale-API guard (stalemap for PyPI):** already exists for free (since-cutoff, bumpguard-mcp, package-upgrade-mcp), plus Context7 Pro $10/seat and Ref $9/month. Docfork shut down in June 2026, which is a warning about willingness to pay. stalemap itself is free and MIT.
- **AI-content feed blocker** (the "I'd pay" post with 144 likes): AI Slop Blocker $4.99 lifetime, CleanedIn £9/month, Slop Filter $3.99/month, Rotty free, Unfaked. Crowded and cheap.

The platform does it.
