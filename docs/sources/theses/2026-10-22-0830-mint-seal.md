# Mint Seal: proof of which coin contract is the official one, from minute zero

*Thesis by idea bot · 22 Oct 2026, 8:30am AWST (Perth time) · status: pitched, awaiting Project Bot review*

## Executive summary
- **Problem:** when a Solana coin takes off, copycat contracts with the same name and ticker appear within minutes, and buyers can't tell which one is real. Kimchi drew 100+ copies and holders still dispute which contract is official; even our own coin tracker may have mislabelled it.
- **Product:** a free "seal" a creator sets up at launch. The deployer wallet signs a statement naming the mint, the project's X account and its website; the X account posts the code and the site hosts a matching file. Mint Seal checks all three, publishes the result as an open on-chain attestation, and answers "is this the official contract?" via a lookup page, an API, an X reply bot and a Telegram bot, with alerts on lookalikes.
- **Who pays:** not the projects (sealing stays free). Paying customers would be launchpads that want a "verified creator" badge at launch, and Telegram trading bots, screeners and wallets that call the API at volume. This is the weakest part and the first thing to test.
- **Why now:** copycat waves are getting faster (100+ "cybercurrency" coins in 20 minutes this morning), projects are improvising their own verification repos and "we have no token" posts, Jupiter has just made an X link optional in its verification flow, and the Solana Attestation Service gives a free, standard place to publish the proof.
- **Edge:** today's checks are either slow and holistic (Jupiter needs market cap, organic trading and social traction, and gives no review timeline unless you burn 1,000 JUP), paid and after the fact (DEX Screener's $299 Enhanced Token Info, no wallet signature), or about risk, not identity (RugCheck, Larpcheck, bitecong). Nobody gives a cryptographic three-way binding (wallet, X account, domain) in the first minutes, when copycats do their damage.
- **Main risk:** revenue and distribution. Jupiter or Pump.fun could make an equivalent check native and free, and the paying customer may not exist outside launchpads.
- **First step:** ship the seal flow and a public lookup page in two weeks, seal 10 real launches by hand, and ask 5 Telegram trading-bot and screener teams whether they'd call the API and pay for volume.

## Problem and who has it
On Solana anyone can mint a token with any name, logo and ticker in seconds. When a coin gets attention, impersonators copy it and push their contract in replies, Telegram call channels and fake airdrops. Buyers looking for "the" contract face several, all claiming to be official.

Who feels it:
- **Buyers and holders** who pick the wrong contract or fall for a lookalike airdrop.
- **Real projects**, whose name gets attached to scams and who have no standard way to say "this one, and only this one." They post one-off notices instead.
- **Tools downstream** (Telegram bots, screeners, trackers like ours) that have to decide which contract to show and sometimes get it wrong.

Projects post their own denials: [Esc Cap: "We have not launched any token"](https://x.com/esccapHQ/status/2112958435994337708), Solpulse "no token yet". - **Git bot:** projects publish one-off "official token verification" repos (for example luckylegend-project/lucky-legend). Git bot's calibration: about five such repos with 0 to 1 stars each, so this shows the behaviour exists, not how big demand is. 0 of 6 tracked AI coins had a linked public repo.
- **Incumbents moving toward the same idea:** Jupiter's application now offers to "connect the project's X account" to "officially link the token's contract address to a specific X account, but it is not required" ([Jupiter docs](https://docs.jup.ag/user-docs/launch/vrfd/token-verification)). Clanker on Base built a trust check for creator claims and says plainly that an X-handle claim "is weaker than Farcaster verification" and should be handled cautiously ([Clanker docs](https://clanker.gitbook.io/documentation/general/verifying-the-social-context-of-token-deploys)).

## Existing solutions and the gap they leave
(Checked on each product's own site or docs this run, per Project Bot's earlier correction.)
- **Jupiter verified list ([docs](https://docs.jup.ag/user-docs/launch/vrfd/token-verification)):** the badge that Phantom and others integrate. Holistic review of market cap (warning under $100k), organic score, holders, ticker uniqueness, liquidity and, most of all, social traction. Standard review is free with no guaranteed timeline; Express costs 1,000 JUP for a 24 to 48 hour review, not a guaranteed pass. Gap: by design it can't help in a coin's first hours, and the X link is optional.
- **DEX Screener Enhanced Token Info ([marketplace](https://marketplace.dexscreener.com/product/token-info)):** $299 to set logo, site and socials. Per a third-party wiki, review checks that the site publishes the token address and links the same socials, with no wallet signature ([Bitsy wiki](https://wiki.bitsy.services/wiki/economics/defi/token-registration/dexscreener/)). Gap: paid, per-screener, not cryptographic, not reusable by other tools.
- **Phantom ([help](https://help.phantom.com/articles/understanding-verified-and-unverified-tokens-in-phantom-38425812822419)):** shows "Verified" when its token-data providers recognise the token, and tells users to check the contract through official project channels. Gap: depends on someone else's list; "official channels" is exactly what's missing.
- **Clanker trust check (Base):** strong when the deployer wallet is a verified address on the creator's Farcaster profile; weak for X. Gap: Base and Farcaster only.
- **Risk and forensics tools:** RugCheck, Larpcheck Bot (public reply scores), CrawlHive (wallet-dump API), bitecong's bundler feed, open-source rug scorers. They answer "is this risky?", not "is this the one the project launched?"
- **Generic wallet-to-X proofs:** Proofable binds a handle to a wallet by signature ([docs](https://docs.proofable.me/verification/accounts)). Gap: not tied to a mint, a domain or launch timing, and not read by trading tools.

**The gap:** a free, instant, signed link between a mint, its project's X account and its domain, set at launch, published in an open format any wallet, bot or screener can read, with active warnings about lookalikes.

## Proposed product and MVP buildable in weeks
**Seal flow (creator side, about 3 minutes):**
1. Connect the deployer or update-authority wallet and sign a message containing the mint, X handle, domain and a timestamp.
2. Post a short code from the X account (or reply to the launch post).
3. Optionally place `/.well-known/token.json` on the domain with the mint and the same code.
4. Mint Seal checks all three and writes an attestation to the [Solana Attestation Service](https://attest.solana.com/docs/guides/ts/how-to-create-digital-credentials), plus a signed JSON record on our API. Partial seals show which links are proven (wallet and X, but no domain).

**Read side (buyer and tool side):**
- Lookup page: paste a contract or a ticker and see "sealed by @handle at 7:01pm, domain verified" or "not sealed; the sealed $KIMCHI is …".
- Public API and an SDK for Telegram bots, screeners and wallets.
- X reply bot ("@mintseal is this the official CA?") and a Telegram bot.
- Lookalike watcher: new mints whose name or ticker matches a sealed coin get flagged as "not the sealed contract," pushed to the project and to subscribers.

**Weeks 1 to 2:** seal flow, SAS write, lookup page, API. **Week 3:** Telegram bot and lookalike watcher. **Week 4:** X reply bot (subject to X API cost) and one launchpad or bot integration.

## Why now
- Copycat speed has outrun manual checking (100+ coins in 20 minutes; X bot).
- Projects are inventing their own fixes (verification repos, "no token" posts, "only this account" announcements), which signals the need for a standard.
- Jupiter's optional X link and Clanker's trust model show the large players accept the idea but haven't solved the launch window on Solana.
- The Solana Attestation Service is live, so the proof can be public and portable rather than locked in one vendor's list.
- Launchpads are taking creator relationships seriously; unverified reports say Pump.fun signed Kimchi (X bot). A launchpad that pays creators has a reason to want their coin to be unmistakable.

## Target customer and go-to-market
- **Free users (supply side):** project teams at launch, reached through launchpad communities and by sealing high-attention launches proactively ("this coin isn't sealed; creator, seal it here").
- **Paying customers (demand side), in order of likely fit:** launchpads (white-label seal at launch, a badge on their own pages); Telegram trading bots and call channels (API at volume, to avoid routing users to fakes); screeners and wallets (API or list feed).
- **Distribution wedge:** the X reply bot and Telegram bot make the answer public in the threads where confusion happens.

## Business model
- Sealing and single lookups free, forever.
- API: free tier, paid tiers by request volume for bots and screeners.
- Launchpad white-label: monthly fee or per-launch fee.
- Project add-on: impersonation monitoring with alerts and ready-to-file reports for X and Telegram.
No pricing data yet; prices are to be set after the interviews below, not assumed.

## Differentiation and moat
- **Cryptographic, not reputational:** a signature from the deployer wallet plus a post from the account, not a judgment about traction.
- **Minute zero:** works before market cap, holders or liquidity exist, which is when the copies hit.
- **Open and portable:** attestations on SAS mean any tool can read them; network effect comes from being the default place creators seal and bots check.
- Moat is weak at first (the flow is easy to copy). It strengthens only if launchpads and big Telegram bots adopt it as the default.

## Risks and how to test them
- **Nobody pays.** Test: 5 to 10 conversations with Telegram trading-bot, screener and launchpad teams before building billing.
- **Jupiter or Pump.fun builds it natively.** Partly a win if they read SAS attestations; pitch as infrastructure they can adopt, not a rival badge.
- **Creators don't bother.** Test: seal 10 live launches by approaching creators directly; measure how many complete the flow.
- **Compromised or rented X accounts** can seal a fake. Mitigate with account age and history shown next to the seal, and alerts when a handle seals more than one mint.
- **Deployer is a launchpad contract, not the creator** (common on Pump.fun). Need to confirm which authority can sign for Pump.fun and Meteora coins; may need the creator's wallet as recorded by the launchpad instead.
- **Legal and liability:** "official" isn't "safe." Every result carries a clear statement that it proves identity, not value.

## 30-day validation plan
- **Days 1 to 3:** confirm, from Pump.fun and Meteora docs and on-chain data, which wallet can credibly sign for a coin; read SAS docs end to end.
- **Days 4 to 14:** build the seal flow, SAS write and lookup page; seal two friendly test coins on devnet, then mainnet.
- **Days 10 to 20:** approach creators of 10 high-attention launches (outreach only with Ethan's approval); target bar, our own threshold: at least 4 of 10 complete a seal.
- **Days 10 to 25:** 5 to 10 interviews with Telegram bot, screener and launchpad teams; bar: at least 2 say they'd integrate the API and 1 names a price.
- **Days 20 to 30:** launch the Telegram lookup bot and lookalike alerts; measure lookups per sealed coin and whether projects share their seal link.
- **Kill if:** fewer than 3 of 10 creators seal and no bot or launchpad team will integrate. **Pivot option:** sell lookalike monitoring directly to real projects.
