# Askwatch: see every public answer @grok gives about your product on X, in real time

*idea bot · 22 Oct 2026, 8:30pm AWST (Perth) · thesis 9 · verdict: **test**, about 35% confidence*

## Executive summary
- **Problem:** On X, people tag @grok under posts to ask "is this legit?", "what's the best tool for X?" or "is this a scam?", and Grok answers in public, in the thread, where every reader sees it. Grok posts about 230,000 public replies a day (X counts API, 16 to 22 Oct). Founders and coin teams can't see these answers, so a wrong price, a "not affiliated" or a "looks like a rug" stands unanswered.
- **Product:** A web app (sign in with X) that indexes the real public @grok replies naming your product, handle, ticker or contract address, plus your competitors. It shows the question asked, Grok's verdict (recommends, neutral or warns), the factual claims it made, reach, and a "reply now" link. Alerts go to Telegram or email within minutes. It complements, and doesn't replace, synthetic-prompt AI visibility trackers.
- **Who pays and how much:** X-native startups, devtools and crypto product teams. Pricing is US$49 a month Starter (1 brand, 3 competitors), US$129 Pro (3 brands, Telegram alerts, API) and US$299 Agency, by card or USDC. Money next door: Rank Prompt has US$43.6k MRR (Stripe-verified on TrustMRR), Elmo US$5.3k and Mentionable US$3.9k, and Peec AI passed US$10M ARR. All of them run synthetic prompts.
- **Token job ($ASKW on pump.fun, after the first paying users):** holders spend it on deep backfills (up to 30 days of history for a term) and stake it for extra tracked terms and faster alert polling. Spent tokens are burned. There are no holder payouts, no revenue buybacks and no profit promises. Creator fees are treated as a bonus.
- **Cost against A$1,000:** about A$470 to the end of a 4-month test, including a small labelled token launch. The US$299 DexScreener profile is paid only from revenue.
- **Path to US$10k MRR:** about 110 paying accounts at a blended US$90 (estimate), sold through free "what Grok told people about you" reports posted on X, TrustMRR and crypto communities.
- **Why now:** Ask-Grok replies are an exposure surface no other model has (Meev says so itself), and since X moved to pay-per-use API pricing (US$0.005 a post read, Feb 2026) one person can afford to index them. Traders in today's digests ask Grok and free bots for legit checks before buying.
- **Edge and main risk:** The edge is real answers seen by real people, not simulated prompts. Meev, Ossvy, Orchly and Gauge run synthetic Grok prompts, Licter does this by hand as a consultancy, and Brand24 (US$199+) catches keywords but doesn't separate out Grok's answers or score them. **The main risk is that it's a thin wedge:** any social-listening tool can add a `from:grok` filter, and nobody has yet shown they'll pay for this one job. **First move this week:** run the free 7-day report for 20 X-active brands by hand and DM nothing; post 3 as public examples only with Ethan's OK.
- **Verdict: test (about 35%).** The money exists next door and the data is cheap, but the paying demand for this narrow job is unproven. Run the 30-day kill test below before building beyond the report page.

## Problem and user
**User:** a tech-bro founder or growth lead at an X-native product. That means an AI tool or devtool with a build-in-public founder, a Solana app or product coin, or a trading tool. Their buyers live on X and increasingly ask @grok instead of Googling.

**What happens today:** Grok answers publicly in threads. Examples from the last week (X search, 22 Oct):
- Grok told one asker a FusedPad post "is legit. It's from the official FusedPad account and matches the live features" ([post](https://x.com/grok/status/2111420073906729384)). That's a free endorsement the team may never have seen.
- Grok described $GROKLET as "fan-made … Unaffiliated with xAI. Multiple similar ones exist" ([post](https://x.com/grok/status/2112231195022909533)).
- Grok judged a trader's account as mixing "solid TA" with "aggressive lowcap meme promotions" ([post](https://x.com/grok/status/2112954900908765475)).
- Grok said it "cannot confirm the free Claude key as legit" and flagged an unofficial proxy (171 impressions, [post](https://x.com/grok/status/2111295566919369006)).

**How they solve it today:** manual X searches, social-listening tools that aren't built for this (Brand24 starts at US$199 a month for 3 keywords, [pricing](https://brand24.com/prices/)), or synthetic-prompt trackers that ask Grok's API a fixed question once a day ([Meev](https://meev.ai/platform/grok-visibility), [Ossvy](https://ossvy.com/pricing), [Orchly](https://orchly.ai/pricing)). Synthetic prompts don't show what Grok actually told the person who asked, under which post, or how many people saw it.

**Cost of the problem:** not measured anywhere I could find. That's the main gap in this thesis, and the kill test is designed to measure it.

## Product and user experience
**First minute:** sign in with X, type a handle, product name, ticker or contract address, and get "Grok answered 37 questions about you in the last 7 days, seen about 4,100 times: 21 neutral, 9 recommend, 7 warn." Under that sits a card per answer showing the question, Grok's reply, the claims extracted (price, "official" or "not affiliated", "scam", competitor named instead) and a reply button that opens the thread. A shareable report image is the growth loop.

**MVP (2 to 3 weeks, solo):**
- **Week 1:** Next.js on Vercel and Supabase (free tiers), X OAuth, an X API pay-per-use worker polling recent search for `from:grok (term1 OR term2…)` and fetching the parent question, and the 7-day free report (sampled and capped).
- **Week 2:** a cheap LLM classifier for verdict, claims and competitor named, with a "wrong fact" check against facts the brand enters (price, official CA, official site). Telegram and email alerts. Stripe Checkout plus a USDC option.
- **Week 3:** competitor share of voice (how often Grok recommends you versus rivals when the question is "best X"), weekly digest, public API.

**Under the hood:** answer clustering by question intent, claim extraction with a fact-diff against the brand's facts, CA-aware matching (so a fake coin's contract can't masquerade as the brand), reach estimates from public impression counts, and dedup to keep X read costs down (X charges once per post per UTC day).

**What's genuinely hard:** keeping X read costs per customer below about 20% of price, avoiding false matches on common words, and getting through X's developer terms review for a commercial monitoring product.

**What a copycat could clone in a weekend:** the basic `from:grok` search with alerts. The moat has to come from the fact-diff, share-of-voice history and distribution on X.

## Token design (pump.fun)
- **Job in the product:**
  - Spend $ASKW on deep backfills (30 days of Grok answers for any term) and one-off competitor audits; spent tokens are burned by the app.
  - Stake (lock) $ASKW to raise a plan's tracked-term cap and alert polling speed while staked.
  - Subscriptions stay payable by card or USDC, so the token is optional.
- **Launch plan:**
  - Launch only after 10 or more paying accounts, on pump.fun, at a public product moment (the first public "Grok report" post).
  - Mint authority off and a small labelled dev buy (about 0.5 SOL). No team bundles. Holder Rewards and Tokenized Agent buybacks off.
  - Post the CA from the official X account within a minute of launch and pin it on the site.
  - DexScreener profile (US$299) paid only from revenue.
- **Fees on-chain:** token burns are visible on-chain, USDC subscriptions go to a published wallet, and the repo is public from day one.
- **Creator fees versus product revenue:** pump.fun creator fees are 0.30% on the curve and 0.30% to 0.95% on PumpSwap at small caps ([pump.fun fees](https://pump.fun/docs/fees)). Trench Bot's data says product use alone doesn't drive token price, so creator fees are a bonus and not in the plan.
- **Comparables:**
  - Larpcheckbot is a reply bot with its own token ($LARPCHECK), but its token's job isn't verified (X bot).
  - Pumpfun Agency's $AGENCY has about US$3.4k mcap and US$43 of 24h volume (Git bot), which shows tool tokens without usage go nowhere.
  - Trench Bot: of 60 coins at US$500k+ only about 9 were real markets, so a product token needs to look unlike a casino coin.
- **Legal line:** no payouts to holders, no revenue-funded buybacks or burns (burns come only from tokens users spend on usage), and no profit language. ASIC and US securities risk get a legal check before launch (budgeted below).

## Defensibility
- **Data history:** a running archive of Grok's answers per brand and category. You can't backfill much beyond 7 days cheaply, so the archive gets more valuable each month.
- **Fact-diff tuned to crypto:** CA, chain, official links and "not affiliated" claims.
- **X-native distribution:** the reports are the content.
- These are weak moats. Speed and the niche are the real defence.

## Kill or confirm (30 days, under A$150)
- **Setup:** build only the free 7-day report page plus Stripe checkout for a US$29 founding plan (alerts delivered by hand where needed). Spend under US$60 on X reads (about 150 free reports).
- **Pass:** 150 or more reports run by distinct users, and **10 or more paid** founding accounts within 30 days of the first public post.
- **Kill:** fewer than 4 paid accounts, or a median under 5 Grok answers a week for the brands that ran reports.
- **In between (4 to 9 paid):** narrow to crypto teams and agencies only and re-test for 2 weeks.
- **Needs Ethan's OK:** any public post or outreach.

## Dropped this run (and why)
- **Creator-reward autopilot** (Git bot's gap): crowded.
  - [Latrine Bot](https://github.com/dfnwtf/latrinebot) is free (claim, buyback, burn, airdrop).
  - [ReFi](https://refi.gg/) takes a 5% fee, [FROZEN](https://github.com/lloydfonterra/frozzzzen) 6% and [AutoBurner](https://github.com/basicapesociety/solana-auto-burner-bot) 5%. [zero-bot](https://github.com/zerocoinsol/zero-bot) does the same.
  - [fees.u.cash](https://fees.u.cash/) already indexes 3,513 fee-routed tokens and 83k claims, and offers self-custody routing.
  - pump.fun's own [creator fee sharing](https://github.com/pump-fun/pump-public-docs/blob/main/docs/instructions/CREATOR_FEE_SHARING.md) splits to up to 10 wallets.
  - Most automated "utility" is buyback, burn or airdrop, which crosses the ASIC line.
- **Coding-agent visibility for devtools:** covered by [Lightsage](https://lightsage.com/docs/guides/track-coding-agents), [Promptwatch](https://promptwatch.com/blog/coding-agent-visibility), [Gauge Agents](https://www.withgauge.com/agents/) and Armature.
- **Key rotation and AI usage visibility:** still crowded (earlier runs).
