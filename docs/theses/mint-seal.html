Mint Seal

Proof of which coin contract is the official one, from minute zero

22 Oct 2026, 8:30am AWST 5/10 Status: Killed pivot to copycat monitoring

Executive summary

  • Problem: when a Solana coin takes off, copycat contracts with the same name and ticker appear within minutes, and buyers can't tell which one is real. Kimchi drew 100+ copies and holders still dispute which contract is official; even our own coin tracker may have mislabelled it.
  • Product: a free "seal" a creator sets up at launch. The deployer wallet signs a statement naming the mint, the project's X account and its website; the X account posts the code and the site hosts a matching file. Mint Seal checks all three, publishes the result as an open on-chain attestation, and answers "is this the official contract?" via a lookup page, an API, an X reply bot and a Telegram bot, with alerts on lookalikes.
  • Who pays: not the projects (sealing stays free). Paying customers would be launchpads that want a "verified creator" badge at launch, and Telegram trading bots, screeners and wallets that call the API at volume. This is the weakest part and the first thing to test.
  • Why now: copycat waves are getting faster (100+ "cybercurrency" coins in 20 minutes this morning), projects are improvising their own verification repos and "we have no token" posts, Jupiter has just made an X link optional in its verification flow, and the Solana Attestation Service gives a free, standard place to publish the proof.
  • Edge: today's checks are either slow and holistic (Jupiter needs market cap, organic trading and social traction, and gives no review timeline unless you burn 1,000 JUP), paid and after the fact (DEX Screener's $299 Enhanced Token Info, no wallet signature), or about risk, not identity (RugCheck, Larpcheck, bitecong). Nobody gives a cryptographic three-way binding (wallet, X account, domain) in the first minutes, when copycats do their damage.
  • Main risk: revenue and distribution. Jupiter or Pump.fun could make an equivalent check native and free, and the paying customer may not exist outside launchpads.
  • First step: ship the seal flow and a public lookup page in two weeks, seal 10 real launches by hand, and ask 5 Telegram trading-bot and screener teams whether they'd call the API and pay for volume.

Test results: Mint Seal kill-or-confirm test: on-chain and public-data half (20 launches)

Full thesis

Thesis by idea bot · 22 Oct 2026, 8:30am AWST (Perth time) · status: pitched, awaiting Project Bot review

Problem and who has it

On Solana anyone can mint a token with any name, logo and ticker in seconds. When a coin gets attention, impersonators copy it and push their contract in replies, Telegram call channels and fake airdrops. Buyers looking for "the" contract face several, all claiming to be official.

Who feels it:

  • Buyers and holders who pick the wrong contract or fall for a lookalike airdrop.
  • Real projects, whose name gets attached to scams and who have no standard way to say "this one, and only this one." They post one-off notices instead.
  • Tools downstream (Telegram bots, screeners, trackers like ours) that have to decide which contract to show and sometimes get it wrong.

Evidence and signals

Corroborated across all three teammates over five runs:

  • Coin tracker (Project Bot): the ledger marks Kimchi 9EQbGT as real and CSocnz as a copycat, but that's now disputed because an X post says Kimchi deployed CSocnz; the creator publishes no official link, so the tracker can't settle it (/workspace/telegram-tracker/coins.md, patterns.md). The same file records a fake "Weft airdrop" contract (qn4E7x…pump) targeting WEFT, plus lure coins with wallet-connect airdrop pages (ChatGBT, COLLECTOOOR).
  • X bot: WEFT holders asked which contract was real for three runs in a row; new Kimchi copycats each run, including 54PyFJ…pump pushed by a 4-follower account and D25wGm…pump with templated "milestone" posts; the "cybercurrency" news word produced 100+ copycat launches in about 20 minutes; a small account claims Kimchi deployed CSocnz (unverified). Projects post their own denials: Esc Cap: "We have not launched any token", Solpulse "no token yet". New coins now pre-announce that "only the official contract will come from this account," and fakes copy that line too (/workspace/x-digests/latest.md and the 06:01 digest).
  • Git bot: projects publish one-off "official token verification" repos (for example luckylegend-project/lucky-legend). Git bot's calibration: about five such repos with 0 to 1 stars each, so this shows the behaviour exists, not how big demand is. 0 of 6 tracked AI coins had a linked public repo.
  • Incumbents moving toward the same idea: Jupiter's application now offers to "connect the project's X account" to "officially link the token's contract address to a specific X account, but it is not required" (Jupiter docs). Clanker on Base built a trust check for creator claims and says plainly that an X-handle claim "is weaker than Farcaster verification" and should be handled cautiously (Clanker docs).

Existing solutions and the gap they leave

(Checked on each product's own site or docs this run, per Project Bot's earlier correction.)

  • Jupiter verified list (docs): the badge that Phantom and others integrate. Holistic review of market cap (warning under $100k), organic score, holders, ticker uniqueness, liquidity and, most of all, social traction. Standard review is free with no guaranteed timeline; Express costs 1,000 JUP for a 24 to 48 hour review, not a guaranteed pass. Gap: by design it can't help in a coin's first hours, and the X link is optional.
  • DEX Screener Enhanced Token Info (marketplace): $299 to set logo, site and socials. Per a third-party wiki, review checks that the site publishes the token address and links the same socials, with no wallet signature (Bitsy wiki). Gap: paid, per-screener, not cryptographic, not reusable by other tools.
  • Phantom (help): shows "Verified" when its token-data providers recognise the token, and tells users to check the contract through official project channels. Gap: depends on someone else's list; "official channels" is exactly what's missing.
  • Clanker trust check (Base): strong when the deployer wallet is a verified address on the creator's Farcaster profile; weak for X. Gap: Base and Farcaster only.
  • Risk and forensics tools: RugCheck, Larpcheck Bot (public reply scores), CrawlHive (wallet-dump API), bitecong's bundler feed, open-source rug scorers. They answer "is this risky?", not "is this the one the project launched?"
  • Generic wallet-to-X proofs: Proofable binds a handle to a wallet by signature (docs). Gap: not tied to a mint, a domain or launch timing, and not read by trading tools.

The gap: a free, instant, signed link between a mint, its project's X account and its domain, set at launch, published in an open format any wallet, bot or screener can read, with active warnings about lookalikes.

Proposed product and MVP buildable in weeks

Seal flow (creator side, about 3 minutes):

  1. Connect the deployer or update-authority wallet and sign a message containing the mint, X handle, domain and a timestamp.
  2. Post a short code from the X account (or reply to the launch post).
  3. Optionally place /.well-known/token.json on the domain with the mint and the same code.
  4. Mint Seal checks all three and writes an attestation to the Solana Attestation Service, plus a signed JSON record on our API. Partial seals show which links are proven (wallet and X, but no domain).

Read side (buyer and tool side):

  • Lookup page: paste a contract or a ticker and see "sealed by @handle at 7:01pm, domain verified" or "not sealed; the sealed $KIMCHI is …".
  • Public API and an SDK for Telegram bots, screeners and wallets.
  • X reply bot ("@mintseal is this the official CA?") and a Telegram bot.
  • Lookalike watcher: new mints whose name or ticker matches a sealed coin get flagged as "not the sealed contract," pushed to the project and to subscribers.

Weeks 1 to 2: seal flow, SAS write, lookup page, API. Week 3: Telegram bot and lookalike watcher. Week 4: X reply bot (subject to X API cost) and one launchpad or bot integration.

Why now

  • Copycat speed has outrun manual checking (100+ coins in 20 minutes; X bot).
  • Projects are inventing their own fixes (verification repos, "no token" posts, "only this account" announcements), which signals the need for a standard.
  • Jupiter's optional X link and Clanker's trust model show the large players accept the idea but haven't solved the launch window on Solana.
  • The Solana Attestation Service is live, so the proof can be public and portable rather than locked in one vendor's list.
  • Launchpads are taking creator relationships seriously; unverified reports say Pump.fun signed Kimchi (X bot). A launchpad that pays creators has a reason to want their coin to be unmistakable.

Target customer and go-to-market

  • Free users (supply side): project teams at launch, reached through launchpad communities and by sealing high-attention launches proactively ("this coin isn't sealed; creator, seal it here").
  • Paying customers (demand side), in order of likely fit: launchpads (white-label seal at launch, a badge on their own pages); Telegram trading bots and call channels (API at volume, to avoid routing users to fakes); screeners and wallets (API or list feed).
  • Distribution wedge: the X reply bot and Telegram bot make the answer public in the threads where confusion happens.

Business model

  • Sealing and single lookups free, forever.
  • API: free tier, paid tiers by request volume for bots and screeners.
  • Launchpad white-label: monthly fee or per-launch fee.
  • Project add-on: impersonation monitoring with alerts and ready-to-file reports for X and Telegram. No pricing data yet; prices are to be set after the interviews below, not assumed.

Differentiation and moat

  • Cryptographic, not reputational: a signature from the deployer wallet plus a post from the account, not a judgment about traction.
  • Minute zero: works before market cap, holders or liquidity exist, which is when the copies hit.
  • Open and portable: attestations on SAS mean any tool can read them; network effect comes from being the default place creators seal and bots check.
  • Moat is weak at first (the flow is easy to copy). It strengthens only if launchpads and big Telegram bots adopt it as the default.

Risks and how to test them

  • Nobody pays. Test: 5 to 10 conversations with Telegram trading-bot, screener and launchpad teams before building billing.
  • Jupiter or Pump.fun builds it natively. Partly a win if they read SAS attestations; pitch as infrastructure they can adopt, not a rival badge.
  • Creators don't bother. Test: seal 10 live launches by approaching creators directly; measure how many complete the flow.
  • Compromised or rented X accounts can seal a fake. Mitigate with account age and history shown next to the seal, and alerts when a handle seals more than one mint.
  • Deployer is a launchpad contract, not the creator (common on Pump.fun). Need to confirm which authority can sign for Pump.fun and Meteora coins; may need the creator's wallet as recorded by the launchpad instead.
  • Legal and liability: "official" isn't "safe." Every result carries a clear statement that it proves identity, not value.

30-day validation plan

  • Days 1 to 3: confirm, from Pump.fun and Meteora docs and on-chain data, which wallet can credibly sign for a coin; read SAS docs end to end.
  • Days 4 to 14: build the seal flow, SAS write and lookup page; seal two friendly test coins on devnet, then mainnet.
  • Days 10 to 20: approach creators of 10 high-attention launches (outreach only with Ethan's approval); target bar, our own threshold: at least 4 of 10 complete a seal.
  • Days 10 to 25: 5 to 10 interviews with Telegram bot, screener and launchpad teams; bar: at least 2 say they'd integrate the API and 1 names a price.
  • Days 20 to 30: launch the Telegram lookup bot and lookalike alerts; measure lookups per sealed coin and whether projects share their seal link.
  • Kill if: fewer than 3 of 10 creators seal and no bot or launchpad team will integrate. Pivot option: sell lookalike monitoring directly to real projects.

Build plan

From “4. Build plan (2 to 4 weeks)”

Re-scope first. Don't lead with "creators seal at launch" (Kimchi shows the creators who matter most won't bother). Lead with the read side that combines every existing proof into one answer, and treat the seal as the upgrade path:

  1. Gather every link that already exists for a mint: Pump.fun or Bags creator wallet and X link, Bags X sign-in, Jupiter verified status, official-account CA posts (like LarpTrack), a /.well-known/token.json file if present, and Mint Seal signatures when available.
  2. Publish the combined result as an SAS attestation with a clear proof level ("X account posted this CA", "creator wallet signed", "domain confirms").

Suggested stack: TypeScript on Node; @solana/attestation (pinned) plus @solana/kit; Helius or Triton RPC with a webhook on Pump.fun and Bags create transactions; Postgres (Supabase is fine); Next.js lookup page on Vercel; grammY for the Telegram bot; X API basic tier only if the reply bot survives week 3 (check the cost before committing).

Features in order

  1. Week 1: indexer for new Pump.fun and Bags launches (creator wallet, ticker, X link from metadata, timestamp) and a same-ticker clone grouper.

  3. Week 2: lookup page and JSON API: paste a CA or ticker, get "most likely official" with the evidence and the proof level.
  4. Week 2: seal flow for creators who want the top proof level (wallet signature, X code, optional domain file), written to SAS.
  5. Week 3: Telegram bot (/ca KIMCHI and paste-a-CA checks) and alerts to subscribers when a sealed coin's ticker gets a new copy.
  6. Week 4: one integration (a launchpad badge or a Telegram trading-bot warning) and a public "unsealed high-attention coins" board.

First 10 users

  • Offer free sealing to the small real projects in the tracker (WEFT is the obvious first ask, since its holders keep asking).
  • Ask 3 Telegram call-channel admins to add the bot to their groups.

The one experiment that kills or confirms it fastest (week 1, before building the seal): take the last 20 high-attention Solana launches from the tracker and X digests and run the resolver by hand (creator wallet, metadata X link, the X account's CA posts). 1. Fact-check

Verified

  • Jupiter's docs say exactly what the thesis says: connecting the project's X account "can help officially link the token's contract address to a specific X account, but it is not required"; social support is "the most important criterion"; Standard review is free with no guaranteed timeline; Express is 1,000 JUP (burned) for a 24 to 48 hour review that is "not verification"; a warning shows under US$100k market cap (Jupiter docs). The thesis left out that anyone, including community members, can apply for any token, and that Jupiter already warns when a ticker is verified for a different contract.
  • DEX Screener Enhanced Token Info costs US$299 (shown as a sale price against US$499), one-off per token. The marketplace page itself doesn't show the price in a plain fetch; the figure comes from third-party pages checked Aug to Sep 2026 (DXT Tools, Dexsheet). Processing is "within just a few minutes, but please allow up to 12 hours" (DEX Screener).
  • Clanker's docs do say a Twitter context match "is weaker than Farcaster verification" and to "handle Twitter verifications cautiously" (Clanker docs).
  • Solana Attestation Service is a Solana Foundation public-good program with credentials, schemas and attestations, including "Social Proof: Verify social media accounts" as a listed use case (SAS docs). Git bot's rename note checks out on npm: @solana/attestation 2.1.0 was published 5 Oct 2026 (a placeholder 0.0.0 on 2 Oct). Watch out: the official docs page still tells you to install sas-lib (latest 1.0.10, with a 2.0.0 beta), so pin the new package and expect stale docs. I couldn't check the 75-star count (no GitHub login on the box).
  • Esc Cap's post says "We have not launched any token" (61 likes at 8:35am AWST) (post).
  • The leext34 post does name CSocnz…pump as the coin Kimchi deployed through Pump.fun; it's a 327-follower account, as X bot said (post).
  • Pump.fun's "create" guide says socials are optional links and that "coins with verified X / Telegram links tend to get more attention" (Pump.fun docs). It doesn't explain what "verified" means there.

Wrong, unsupported or out of date

  • "Nobody gives a cryptographic three-way binding … in the first minutes." Partly wrong. Lattice's whitepaper describes project self-verification where "proof of control is a signed message … from the deployer or owner wallet, or a one-time code posted from the project's official account", after which check @handle resolves to the contract with a Verified Project badge. Its X bot (@latticehoodbot) is suspended as of this run and the site is a one-line landing page, so it looks stalled or dead. That's useful: it's the same idea, tried, with no visible traction.
  • "Risk and forensics tools … answer 'is this risky?', not 'is this the one the project launched?'" Wrong for LarpTrack. Its methodology (draft v0.3, 17 Oct 2026) resolves the official CA from the X account in the token's own metadata, checks that account's latest CA post, grades same-ticker mints as "likely clone" (F), and runs a recorder that re-reads every official account every 15 minutes and catches deleted posts (LarpTrack methodology). It's free, with a bot reply and a shareable card. It's small (112 followers, account created 17 Oct, has its own token).
  • Missing incumbent: FakeWatch. Clone-token monitoring on DEXes, a public verification page, a /verify bot for Telegram and Discord, lookalike domains, X impersonators and takedown reports. Free plan, then US$39, US$99 and US$349 a month (the top plan is pitched at "launchpads, agencies and studios"), paid in USDT or USDC (FakeWatch pricing). That's the thesis's "lookalike watcher" and "impersonation monitoring add-on", already priced. Its examples are mostly EVM addresses, so Solana depth is unconfirmed. Tiny X presence (6 followers).
  • Missing incumbent: Bags. Creators verify ownership by connecting an X, TikTok or Instagram account, and the Bags API returns each mint's creator with provider and username (/token-launch/creator/v3) (Bags how it works, Bags API docs). For Bags coins, the mint-to-X link is already proven at launch by account sign-in, which is stronger than a posted code. It's locked inside Bags (API key needed), which is exactly the "not portable" gap.
  • "Even our own coin tracker may have mislabelled it." Understated: it very likely has. Pump.fun's public coin data for CSocnzcRpgSCr1naKcNTieWZUxb6rABe45j844m7pump shows ticker "Kimchi", X link kimchi1x, creator wallet EFSr39…D56oF, created 23:00:15 UTC on 20 Oct (7:00:15am AWST 21 Oct), and about US$24.7M market cap at this run. The Defiant puts the Kimchi mint at "7:00:15 pm" ET Tuesday, the same second (The Defiant, post). The X link is self-declared metadata, so this is strong evidence, not proof. Action for the tracker: flip CSocnz to "likely real" and re-check 9EQbGT.
  • Kimchi's own account never posted a contract. His last three posts (15 to 21 Oct) are a link, a vlog post and "@Pumpfun let's work" (kimchi1x). That supports the thesis (the creator gave no official link), but note it would also defeat Mint Seal: a creator who never posts a CA won't run a seal flow either.

The room's focus points

  • Can a Pump.fun creator wallet sign the statement? Yes, in practice. Pump.fun's coin data names a creator wallet per mint (the wallet that signed the launch transaction; The Defiant's "3-hr old wallet"), separate from the launchpad program. That wallet can sign a message, so the seal can bind to "the creator recorded by Pump.fun" rather than the mint or update authority. Two catches: the creator can be a throwaway wallet with no history (as with Kimchi), so the signature proves "same person who launched", not "the person on X"; and Pump.fun's coin API isn't a documented public API (LarpTrack notes it needs browser-like headers), so build on on-chain create transactions as the source of truth.
  • Who pays? Still unanswered, and the evidence leans against bots paying. FakeWatch's market is projects paying monthly; Jupiter and DEX Screener charge projects one-off; LarpTrack, Larpcheck and Lattice are free (or token-funded). I found no example of a trading bot or screener paying a third party for "official CA" data. Pump.fun reportedly paying Kimchi (about US$6.5M in PUMP, unverified, X bot) shows launchpads do care which coin is "theirs", but a launchpad that already has the creator wallet and X link can badge it itself.
