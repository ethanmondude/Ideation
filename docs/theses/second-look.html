Second Look

A pre-refund check that AI support agents run before paying out on a "damaged item" photo

22 Oct 2026, 12:30pm AWST 5/10 Status: Pitched, awaiting Project Bot review

Executive summary

  • Problem: Shopify brands refund or replace "arrived damaged" orders on the strength of a photo, and anyone can now fake that photo in seconds with ChatGPT or Gemini. Worse, support is being handed to AI agents. Gorgias's own docs suggest telling its AI Agent: "If the image clearly shows a damaged item… offer to send a replacement or issue a refund… without escalating to a human". A faked photo can now get a refund with no person ever looking at it.
  • Product: a Gorgias + Shopify app. It runs before any damage refund or replacement, whether an AI agent or a person is handling the ticket. It returns allow / step-up / hand over in under 5 seconds, based on: forensic checks on the photo (bought in from a detection API, not built), a match of the photo against the ordered product, a duplicate check against the store's past claim photos, and the customer's Shopify claim and refund history. "Step-up" sends a one-tap live-photo link, so honest customers only get friction when there are signs of fraud. Bonus hook: a 90-day backfill audit of the claim photos already in Gorgias.
  • Who pays and how much: Shopify brands on Gorgias, especially those with AI Agent switched on. Billed through Shopify: Free (25 checks a month), Starter $39, Growth $99, Scale $249 a month. That's in line with Verilo ($29/$79/$199), Claimscan (€89) and PixelProof ($19), and far below Wyllo/Yofi (25% of fraud prevented, or from $250 a month).
  • Path to $10k a month: about 100 paying brands at around $99. That's roughly 4% of an estimated 2,500 Gorgias brands with AI Agent on and photo-based refunds (estimate; arithmetic below). Customers come from the Gorgias and Shopify app stores, a free web photo checker, and the backfill audit as the moment that converts. Base case is about $9k MRR by month 12. Reaching $10k needs month 12 or a Zendesk/Fin extension.
  • Why now: Ravelin's survey of 6,200 shoppers: 65% say AI has made fake refund claims easier (Jan 2026). Yofi's CEO: "exploded overnight… daily across our merchants" (Modern Retail). Boll & Branch and Bogg have publicly caught AI-faked damage photos. At the same time, AI agents are being given refund authority (Gorgias AI Agent Vision plus Actions). Gorgias custom Actions can call an outside HTTP endpoint mid-conversation, so the integration route exists today.
  • Edge (narrow; be honest): at least 10 tools check claim photos. All of them sit at the customer's capture step or upload portal (Verilo, PixelProof, ReturnsIQ, Sorquil), or are an API with connectors still "coming soon" (Truvane), or live in another helpdesk (Zendesk's Sentinel, eDesk). I found none that plugs into the AI agent's refund decision inside Gorgias, combines photo forensics with the customer's Shopify history, and back-scans past tickets. Gorgias also bills $0.90 to $1.00 per automated interaction, so it has a soft reason not to build a feature that hands tickets back to humans.
  • Main risk: this is a crowded, fast-forming category. Truvane or Verilo could ship a Gorgias Action within weeks, or Gorgias could add forensics to Vision. Fakes may also be too rare for small brands to keep paying ("never caught anything" churn).
  • First move this week: ship the free web photo checker plus a copy-paste Gorgias Action template (4 to 5 days), backed by Sightengine's AI-image API. Then put a $49 founding-plan Stripe link in front of 30 Gorgias brands' CX leads. Verdict: TEST, not build. Confidence about 40%. Build the full app only if at least 5 brands pay within 30 days (thresholds below).

Problem and buyer

Persona. Head of CX or founder-operator at a Shopify brand doing roughly $1M to $30M GMV a year. They run Gorgias (most of these brands are on the Pro plan, which Gorgias says has "12,400+ brands" (Gorgias pricing)) and have switched on, or are trialling, Gorgias AI Agent at $0.90 to $1.00 per automated interaction (Gorgias AI Agent pricing). They sell physical goods where a refund or reship without a return is common: linens, homewares, glass and ceramics, beauty, apparel, bulky items.

What they do today (sourced):

  • They trust the photo. "Like many online retailers, Boll & Branch… typically ship[s] a new item as soon as a customer provides a photo of any product defects" (Modern Retail).
  • They hand it to the AI agent. Gorgias tells merchants to give AI Agent guidance such as "If the image clearly shows a damaged item… offer to send a replacement or issue a refund based on our refund policy", explaining that "the AI can now verify that the product is indeed damaged and act according to your policy without escalating to a human" (Gorgias docs). The Vision feature reads images. Nothing in the doc says it checks whether they are authentic.
  • When they suspect fraud, they improvise: a FaceTime call (Boll & Branch), standard "send it back first" wording (Bogg), or forced returns. All of these add friction for honest customers. Bogg: "it ruins it for everybody else" (Modern Retail).

What it costs them (sourced where possible):

  • Industry level: about 14% of retail returns are considered fraudulent, more than $100B a year (NRF and Happy Returns, quoted by Modern Retail). E-commerce returns are expected to cost brands $379B in 2026 (eMarketer, same article). These are all returns-fraud types, not just photo claims.
  • Per incident: Boll & Branch duvet sets cost $489. Order Protection saw a faked claim on $600+ sneakers (Modern Retail). Claimscan quotes an "average fake claim: €180" (Claimscan, vendor figure). Sorquil's illustration uses $85 (Sorquil).
  • Frequency at a mid-size brand is low for now. Bogg sees "a couple of times a month". Boll & Branch found "a handful", and its CEO said "there's probably some that got through that we didn't realize" (Modern Retail). Experts expect it to grow: "In another six months, this is going to be a bigger problem" (Signifyd's CCO, same article).
  • Chargeback tail: Ravelin notes fraudsters reuse the same fake evidence in a card dispute if the merchant refuses (Ravelin).

What's left (the gap, stated narrowly): a pre-refund decision endpoint inside Gorgias that its AI Agent (or a human macro) calls. It combines (a) forensics on photos already received, (b) a photo-vs-ordered-product match, (c) store-level duplicate detection, and (d) the customer's Shopify claim history. It returns allow, step-up or hand over, with step-up live capture only when something looks wrong, plus a backfill audit of past tickets. None of the products above does that combination today, as far as I could find on their own pages (checked 22 Oct). Each piece exists somewhere, so the moat is integration and speed, not technology.

Why incumbents may not build it (soft reasons, not guarantees): Gorgias earns per automated interaction, so a feature that hands suspicious refunds back to humans works against its headline metric, though it could still ship one to protect merchants. The fraud platforms (Wyllo, Signifyd, Ravelin) sell contingency or enterprise contracts and won't price at $39 to $99. The live-capture apps would have to give up their "no uploads" pitch to analyse passively.

Product

MVP (web stack Ethan already uses): Shopify embedded app (Remix/React Router template, Polaris), Postgres (Neon or Supabase), a queue (Inngest or a simple worker), Cloudflare R2 for short-lived images (deleted after 30 days; perceptual hashes kept), Stripe for the pre-launch founding link, then Shopify Billing API.

  • Detection: Sightengine's AI-generated image model (5 operations per image: $29 a month buys 10,000 operations, or about 2,000 checks; overage $0.002 per operation, about $0.01 per check) (Sightengine pricing, operations FAQ). Add EXIF and content-credential (C2PA) parsing and pHash duplicate detection (open source). Keep the detection provider swappable (Truvane, Hive) so accuracy can be bought, not built.
  • Product match: one vision-LLM call: "does this photo show the ordered SKU (compare with the store's product images)? Is the damage consistent with the material?" Est. under $0.005 per image on a Flash-class model (estimate).
  • Customer history: Shopify Admin API for orders, refunds and tags, giving prior claims, refund count, days since delivery, first order, and the same address used across accounts.
  • Gorgias integration: (1) a custom HTTP Action template for AI Agent: "Verify damage claim" → JSON {decision, reasons[], stepup_url}, with conditions branching on decision (Gorgias supports HTTP steps and JSONPath mapping: Gorgias docs); (2) an HTTP integration and sidebar widget for human agents; (3) a backfill via the Gorgias REST API that scans attachments on tickets tagged damaged or defective from the last 90 days.
  • Step-up page: a mobile web page that takes 3 live photos through the browser camera (no gallery upload) plus an order-label photo, with a timestamp and server-issued nonce shown on screen. This is a deliberately light version of Verilo, and only used when a claim is flagged.

Week-by-week (solo, using AI coding tools):

  1. Week 1: free web checker (upload a photo → report and PDF), detection pipeline, landing page, Stripe founding link. Gorgias HTTP Action template, documented.
  2. Week 2: Shopify embedded app (OAuth, order and customer history scoring), decision API, internal-note writer for Gorgias.
  3. Week 3: step-up live-capture page, backfill audit (Gorgias API), dashboard (claims checked, flagged, step-ups abandoned, $ held).
  4. Week 4: Shopify Billing, App Store listing assets, Gorgias App Store submission, docs. Submit (Shopify review takes weeks, so sell through the founding link and the Action template meanwhile).

What's hard: (1) false positives. Real photos that were compressed, cropped or edited for brightness can look edited. Mitigation: never auto-deny, only step-up or hand over. Calibrate on the merchant's own backfill. (2) Getting the Gorgias Action to run reliably before AI Agent commits to a refund. Its irreversible actions already require customer confirmation, so insert the check before that step. Test in a real Gorgias trial in week 1. (3) App review lead times on two marketplaces.

What a copycat clones in a weekend: the upload checker and the detection API wrapper. What takes longer: the Gorgias Action plus Shopify-history scoring plus step-up plus backfill working together, App Store reviews and ranking, and calibration data from real step-up outcomes.

Defensibility

Low to moderate, and the thesis doesn't pretend otherwise.

  1. Integration depth and switching cost: once the Action is wired into a brand's AI Agent Guidance and macros, ripping it out is real work.
  2. Calibration data: every step-up outcome (completed or abandoned) is a label. Over time that gives per-category thresholds a new entrant can't copy.
  3. Opt-in cross-store photo hash list: perceptual hashes only, no images. A photo or template reused across stores gets flagged. Forrester's Kodali recommends sharing data on repeat bad actors (Modern Retail). It's weak at 10 stores, meaningful at a few hundred.
  4. Reviews and ranking in two app stores. In Shopify, early reviews compound.

Kill or confirm: 30-day paid validation

Run before building billing or the App Store listings. All outreach needs Ethan's explicit approval and goes out from his own accounts.

  • Build (days 1 to 5): free web checker, Gorgias HTTP Action template, backfill script (run with the merchant's API key on a call or self-serve), $49 a month founding Stripe link.
  • Measure (days 6 to 30):
  • ≥ 30 Gorgias-brand CX leads contacted; ≥ 12 backfill audits completed.
  • Flag rate on real claim photos ≥ 2 per 100 claims across the audits, with at least 3 flags the merchant agrees were likely fake.
  • Step-up rate on photos the merchant confirms are real ≤ 3%.
  • ≥ 5 brands pay $49 or more by card (target ≥ 8).
  • ≥ 2 brands wire the Action into AI Agent or a live macro.
  • Confirm (build the full app) if paid ≥ 5 and merchant-agreed fakes ≥ 3.
  • Kill if paid ≤ 1, or fewer than 1 likely fake per 100 claims across 12 audits (fraud too rare to sell), or real-photo step-ups above 8%.
  • Pivot if audits show fakes but nobody pays a monthly fee: offer it as a $199 one-off audit, or as a contingency fee (Yofi-style 20%). (These thresholds are our own estimates, not industry benchmarks.)
