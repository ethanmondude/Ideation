Executive summary

  • Problem: the scam sits between an X post and a buy. Trench Bot's log has 856 rugs among 869 coins that crossed US$500k. Run 24 alone was 27 rugs out of 28. The brand-bait dump template (one 13–14% wallet plus a 0.24% companion) came back with the same two wallets.
  • Product: Marshal is a free, read-only Chrome extension for x.com. It folds paid-caller replies with a reason, puts a fact pill (never green) by every Solana CA, and warns when the coin page you open isn't the CA the post wrote. It names the earlier rug a coin's top wallet already dumped. The stamp is grey until the gate, and it never fires on dust, pool authorities or operators that also hold live coins.
  • Who pays and how much: users pay nothing (Ethan: free only). Income is a disclosed, optional Axiom referral link (30% of Axiom's net fee on referred users, docs). Creator fees are a bonus.
  • Token's job: v1.1 only. Projects or named people burn their own $MRSHL to register their real CA or "no coin", and users burn ≥US$2 of their own to file a report. No payouts, buybacks or holder unlocks. Token fit 6/10 (Project Bot).
  • Cost against A$1,000: ~A$73 to day 30. A$449 lean / A$549 worst over 4 months, including ~A$300 legal (est.). Running cost is capped at A$25 a month, and v6.7 adds A$0.
  • Path to US$10k/month: about 667 active referred Axiom traders at US$15 a month each (est.). Base case at month 12 is US$1.5–3.4k a month (est.).
  • Why now, edge, main risk: pump.fun is still ~79% of 24h US$500k coins (100% of the ones with real liquidity), the boycott is fading, and repeat dump wallets are stable. The edge is automatic, deterministic folds plus on-chain stamps under every post, with no tag and no wallet connection. Main risks: XRAY or SlipZero adding reply folds, and referral income never covering costs.
  • First move this week: Ethan picks the name. Claude Code then builds from SPEC 1549aeeb…13d6 (final). Trench Bot's 14:00 run decides r4b_red. The 30-day unlisted listing is the referral test.
  • Verdict: build, about 50% confident (unchanged). My score estimate is 7.0, rising to 7.25 if the 14:00 run keeps 0 false stamps and red turns on. XRAY is a downside risk to Uniqueness (6) that Project Bot should weigh.

I started from X bot's 11:59 lead: cheap one-time-price clones of pricey SaaS (DataBull US$29 lifetime, rising to US$99; Beacon US$197 once). I looked for a crypto-native version that suits tech-bro traders and a pump.fun token.

  • Pay-once memecoin terminal: gates c + e. MegaBot Pro sells US$999 lifetime, Krypto Bot is free, and ZER◎ sells one-time setup. The clones' selling features are bundlers and volume bots, which is market manipulation Ethan shouldn't sell.
  • "Which post moved my coin" attribution for coin teams (DataBull for pump.fun): gates a + c. The buyers are coin teams, not traders, and 856 of 869 US$500k coins are rugs. Weavr, web3trackers, Mintfunnel, Kaizen and XRAY already ship it.
  • Pay-once Frontrun-style X-profile intel extension: gates c + f. Frontrun is free with 6M labels and open source, XRAY exists, and the moat is X history data that A$1,000 can't buy.
  • Fee-recipient "claim vs chain" check (a post says "fees go to @x / your FOMO wallet", and we compare it with the on-chain sharing config): this angle isn't covered by SlipZero, creator-rewards or pump.fun's skill, because they look up recipients but never compare against the post's claim. But it only makes sense under the post, which makes it Marshal R13b, not a separate product. Stand-alone: gates b + d (no token job, no payer). Kept in SPEC §12.3 v1.1 with the wallet-recipient wording.
  • Pay-once AI-search-visibility audit (US$79 anchors): gates a + c. Askwatch is parked, and ProofSource and QAS sell it already.
  • Pay-once agent audit-trail/replay ("would pay 3x for replay"): gates b + c. Langfuse, AgentOps and Helicone are free/OSS, and there's no pump.fun token job.
  • Count: 7 dropped (a+c ×2, b+c ×2, c+e ×1, c+f ×1, b+d ×1). No second thesis written. Pattern: one-time clones work where the incumbent's moat is software. Crypto trader tools' moats are data (labels, X history) or execution, and free self-host versions already exist.
