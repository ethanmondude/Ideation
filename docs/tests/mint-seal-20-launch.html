Method

  • Sample (20): 11 tracker coins detected on 21 Oct, taken from /workspace/telegram-tracker/coins.md (Kimchi counted once). I left out TSLA and META because their first pool dates from June 2026, so they aren't new launches. The other 9 were the top launches from roughly the last 48 hours in Jupiter's public 24h lists (toporganicscore, toptraded, toptrending), picked on volume, holders and organic score. I ranked on those rather than market cap, because several Pump.fun "market caps" look inflated (see HELL and the second Kimchi wave). WEFT is added at the end as an extra case outside the window. It is not counted in the tally.
  • Window caveat: FAUCET, ENERGY and NOX were detected on 21 Oct but created on 19 Oct (about 58 to 78 hours ago), so they fall outside a strict 48-hour window.
  • Sources (public, read-only, no sign-ins): Pump.fun's public coin endpoints (frontend-api-v3.pump.fun/coins-v2/<mint> and /coins/search-v2), which work with browser-style headers. These are undocumented, so treat them as fragile. Also used: Jupiter Tokens API v2 (lite-api.jup.ag/tokens/v2/search), the DEX Screener public API (/tokens/v1/solana/<mint>, /latest/dex/search), and each project's own website, including its JS bundles, searched for a contract address.
  • Same-ticker count: Solana mints whose symbol matches the ticker exactly (case-insensitive) or whose name matches exactly, created within ±24h of the target. EVM 0x results from Pump.fun's multichain search are excluded. These counts are lower bounds. Pump.fun search was capped at 1,000 results, the Jupiter and DEX Screener searches return only about 20 to 30 each, and look-alike tickers (for example CIMCHI or KIMCHIBULL) are not counted. X bot counted 100+ copies for Kimchi and for the "cybercurrency" wave. My exact-match counts are much smaller.
  • Creator-listed socials: the X account and website in the token's own metadata, as returned by Pump.fun or Jupiter. DEX Screener's "Enhanced Token Info" socials are paid for by whoever buys them, so they are noted separately as "DS paid".
  • Holders, volume and market cap: Jupiter snapshot at about 8:40am AWST, 22 Oct. Volume on many copycat pools is clearly wash-traded: millions in 24h volume with 10 to 50 holders and zero liquidity. So holders are used as the attention signal, not volume.

Classification rule (written before tallying):

  • Clear (A, confirmed): the project's own website names the contract, or a launchpad or aggregator verification flag (Pump.fun verified: true or Jupiter isVerified) names it, and nothing public contradicts that.
  • Clear (B, dominance only): there's no site or flag confirmation, but one contract has at least 3 times the holders of the next same-ticker, same-narrative contract in the window, and no site or flag points elsewhere.
  • Ambiguous: two or more same-narrative contracts have holder counts within 3 times of each other, or public sources point to different contracts.
  • Unknown: couldn't be resolved. None ended up in this bucket.

"Owner" below means a project or person who could sign a seal: a real project X account, a site, or a named creator. "Ownerless" means a narrative or news coin whose metadata X link is just someone's tweet about an event, so there is nobody to seal it.

X merge (pending)

The merge rule is: "already clear" only if public data and the project's own X post agree on the same contract. Otherwise the launch counts as ambiguous. X bot's results weren't in latest.md when I finished. What can already be predicted:

  • The 6 ownerless coins (CUPSEY 21 Oct wave, PHP, CYBER, PHP2SHELL, HUMANOID, 4), plus FAUCET, whose metadata has no X and whose paid X link is only a community, not an account, can't have a "project's own X post". Read literally, they all become ambiguous.
  • Kimchi becomes ambiguous. The review already found that @kimchi1x never posted a contract.
  • So under the literal rule, the final tally can't be higher than about 12 of 20 clear, well under 18. That would read as "confirm, the seal has a job". I don't think that reading is right (next section).

Verdict

Leaning kill-or-pivot for the seal, and keep the monitoring angle. The 17 of 20 headline undersells how clear things are where a seal could actually help:

  1. Among the 14 launches with an owner who could sign (NOMO, FAUCET, ENERGY, TRUMP3, NOX, Kimchi, RAPR2, LASTAGENCY, PULLBACK, MONEYBIZ, JONATHAN, shiit, FORK, HELL), public data was clear in 14 of 14. In 10 of those, the project's own site or a launchpad or Jupiter flag named the contract. A seal would only restate it.
  2. All 3 ambiguous cases are ownerless news or narrative coins (PHP, CYBER, PHP2SHELL). There's nobody to sign a seal, so a seal can't fix them. Counting them as evidence for Mint Seal would be wrong. The literal X-merge rule falls into exactly that trap, so I suggest X bot's results are read separately for owned and ownerless coins.
  3. Kimchi, the thesis's headline case, is now clear from public data (Pump.fun's verified flag, earliest, about 10 times the holders of any rival). Its creator never posted a contract, so he wouldn't have run a seal flow either.
  4. What public data does show is a real "the copy looks bigger" problem, even when the official site is clear: HELL, WEFT and JONATHAN copies with more holders, inflated market caps sitting above the real coin on Pump.fun, wash-volume copies at the top of volume rankings (PULLBACK, HUMANOID, JONATHAN), copies reusing the official X and site (NOMO, RAPR2, TRUMP3, PULLBACK, MONEYBIZ, JONATHAN, shiit, HELL, WEFT), and a copy listing a different X handle (MONEYBIZ). That's a job for monitoring and alerts for projects (the FakeWatch or LarpTrack lane), plus maybe a resolver that weighs the site and flags over holders or market cap. It isn't a job that needs a new signature at launch.

Recommendation: treat this as a fail for "creators seal at launch". Unless X bot's 9:55am results show owned projects whose own X posts disagree with public data, pivot Mint Seal to the review's "read side that combines every existing proof" plus copycat monitoring sold to projects.

Caveats

  • Pump.fun's endpoints are undocumented. Its verified flag isn't explained publicly (Pump.fun docs only mention "verified X / Telegram links"), so I treat it as a signal, not proof.
  • Holder counts can be inflated by airdropped dust, which may explain 4ydpbM and HFgAnk. Market caps on thin pools can be pushed up cheaply.
  • I classified each coin as of 8:40am AWST, with hindsight. Several cases, Kimchi most of all, were murkier in the first minutes to hours, which is when buyers actually choose. This test can't measure first-hour ambiguity. If idea bot wants to rescue the seal, that's the remaining argument.
  • I didn't open X, Telegram, Solscan pages or any signed-in source. Solscan and DEX Screener links are given for readers to check.
